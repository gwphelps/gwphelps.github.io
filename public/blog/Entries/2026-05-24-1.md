## Bakemonogatari Light Novel Vol 2

I recently finished Vol 2 of the Bakemonogatari LN. I have a long tweet chain with some of my more sporadic thoughts as I read, but I wanted to try to conjur up something more coherent here. Overall I did not by any means think the novel was significantly better than the anime. In general Studio Shaft does a ton to elevate the dialogue from the novel into a visually distinct and interesting style. Most of the dialogue is pretty much 1-to-1 from the novel, with some cut content here and there. So the point of this is less to sing the novel's praises per say, but more to point out things I noticed that were different or interesting. 

### Suruga Monkey
It's been a little while since I read Suruga Monkey at this point, but I think it was very similar to it's anime counterpart. While Nadeko Snake contrasts Kanbaru and Senjougahara, this arc contrasts Kanbaru and Araragi. Araragi really puts Kanbaru on a pedestal, as if she's someone a nobody like him would never have any cause to interact with. It's a little more explicit in the novel that Araragi thinks Kanbaru is more worthy of Senjougahara than he is, not that it really matters since Senjougahara is not into Kanbaru, but it's part of the calculus that results in him deciding to let Kanbaru kill him. 

The fight scene if I'm honest is just much better in the anime. It was very interesting to hear Araragi's thoughts though. In the anime it's not clear if Araragi intended to let Kanbaru kill him from the beginning or not. In the novel it's clear that he made that decision mid-fight. 

Outside of that there were several references to important plot points that I think were not in the anime:
- Explicit mention of the Gaen family by Oshino, as well as alluding to something ominous about the lineage. 
- Araragi admits that he is jealous of Hanekawa's virtuous nature, one of the big points of contention in Neko Kuro, and heavily related to Araragi feelings of fakeness which get set up in Nise.

### Nadeko Snake

Nadeko Snake is probably the first arc that I thought was just straight up better than the anime. Nadeko Snake is widely considered the worst arc in Monogatari, due to being pretty thematically uninteresting, and having maybe the weakest female lead, Nadeko. The novel, however, brings to the forefront some of the major ideas being explored in the arc in more detail, while they are really only vaguely mentioned in the anime. These ideas would be Araragi's indiscriminate kindness, and his wavering feelings about Senjougahara. 

Probably the most significate change between the anime and the novel is at least half an episode's worth of cut banter between Kanbaru and Araragi as they are about the start climbing the mountain for the first time. The main point of the banter is that Kanbaru keeps hitting on Araragi, and while he is resisting it well, internally it is affecting him, and endearing him to Kanbaru in a way he did not expect. She keeps talking about how they are on a date, even though Araragi denies this. This adds even more weight to the scene at the library when Hanekawa pretends to try to kiss Araragi, and then chastises him for allowing Kanbaru to get so close. Their dynamic seemed a little more harmless in the anime, but in the novel it's very explicit that Kanbaru is trying, mostly as a joke, to act like a girlfriend to Araragi. It's just more evidence that Araragi is not as committed to Senjougahara as he perhaps should be. There is also a line where Araragi even mentions that he finds Kanbaru very easy to talk to and banter with, in contrast to Senjougahara who is more difficult to talk to, to put it mildly. 

If you did not come to understand Araragi's insane desire to help people when he decides to help Kanbaru in Suruga Monkey, a woman who actually tried to kill him, it is supposed to be pretty explicit in this arc, when Araragi laments that he was not able to prevent the snake from going back to the boy who sent it. Since we get Araragi's inner thoughts here his torment over this situation is very apparent, and also his disgust in himself for feeling that way. 

### Miscellaneous
Some miscellaneous things I noticed: Araragi mentions that he doesn't remember middle school (the time when Nadeko was at their house frequently) very well. Araragi's "forgetfulness" in Owari s1 is one of the parts of Monogatari I like less and it seems like they are setting it up much, much better in the novels. In the anime it felt like it came out of nowhere to me.  

Araragi also mentions that he thinks he has changed completely since middle school. We probably won't get any payoff for this until Owari but if I remember correctly this is probably related to the incident in 9th grade that shattered his sense of justice, paired with falling behind in school. His transformation from studious, morally upstanding kid to apathetic slacker burnout. 

Kanbaru also implicitly confirms that at least part of the purpose of her sexual banter with Araragi is to try to sabotage his relationship with Senjougahara. I don't think they ever state it that clearly in the anime but I always assumed that. 

There is a line about how Hanekawa, Kanbaru, and Mayoi were unable to go back to normal after their oddity incidents. But Senjougahara was. And I think that's a very real aspect of the story, because Araragi makes a point to not involve Senjougahara in his oddity stuff (Though she involves herself sometimes). This to me is related to how Senjougahara and Shinobu never talk to each other, because Shinobu represents the "vampire" aspect of Araragi, aka these monstrous qualities that he doesn't want to show his girlfriend. I don't know if I'm explaining it well but that idea is what this line makes me think of. I'll probably get around to expanding on that somewhere else.

Araragi also thinks that Hanekawa's desire to travel is due to her encounter with oddities making the straight and narrow path less appealing to her. Which is probably true to an extent but I think may also be projection on Araragi's part. I think the events of Kizu made HIM find the typical life path less appealing, and we find him meditating on this very idea in Koyomimonogatari, which I am excited to read since I have already read the chapter 1's that were omitted from the anime and though they were great, great stuff.  

Kanbaru mentions that Nadeko seems like someone who would be prone to self-harm, a one-off line that will probably become important later. I never really connected Nadeko to self-harm while watching, but once it was pointed out to me I could definitely see it. Let's see if it's more explicit in the novels or not. 

### Overall
Overall, I'd say this novel was not significantly better or worse than the anime. It has more details, but I don't think the show runners were insane for cutting most of the Kanbaru dialogue in Nadeko Snake, which was the only major difference I noticed.
