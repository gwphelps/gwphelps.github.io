## Why Won't They Just Get Together Already?

Lately I've been thinking a lot about the timing of the confession in romance anime. As a westerner it's hard for me to know how much of the bombastic confession of love from anime is based on reality, and how much of it is self-referential trope. But regardless there is definitely an artform to both the timing and the execution. I honestly haven't even seen that many romances/romcoms to really be any sort of authority on the subject, but since it's on my mind I want to get my thoughts on the subject out there.

### 1. Just Confess Already! (aka Milking it for Content)
This is by far the most common problem with romance development in anime: They take way too long to actually confess! This can fall into a number of categories, most notably stagnation and regression. 

In a stagnated romance the actual romance will take a backseat to other plot points, drama between friends for example. My Dress Up Darling is guilty of this, focusing more on the side characters in the second season to slow down the relationship progression. In episodic shows you can also get an issue where the relationship progress essentially resets at the beginning of each episode, regardless of what actually happens in the episode itself. I personally haven't seen any like this but I've heard older romcoms tend to be like this. 

A so-called "slow burn" romance is not inherently bad though, the main issue is the lack of development. So if the author can provide satisfying relationship progression on a consistent basis, the confession itself does not need to be rushed necessarily just for the sake of it. I would classify The Dangers in my Heart as this type of show. The confession happens after 2 seasons, which is not an insanely long time but depending on the execution could be a bit of a drag. But they make it work by providing continuous development in the main couple from strangers, to friends, to more than friends over a longer period of time. 

### 2. Wait, He/She Confessed Already??
This is a less common problem than the previous but I still think it's important to talk about. A decent number of anime coming out recently have very fast confessions, some as early as the first episode. And depending on the type of show it can be fine to spend more time on the relationship itself once it begins, but if you are trying to do the best possible love story there is a lot of meat to be found in the space between friends and dating that stories like that aren't exploring enough. 

What comes to mind to me is the friends-to-lovers trope. The best part of that type of story is the conflict between how much the two characters like their current dynamic and the romantic tension pulling them into new territory almost against their will. If you get them to start dating too early you miss out on the impact of drawing it out until neither of them can deny their feelings anymore. 

Horimiya is my poster child for the too-fast confession. I feel like I barely know these characters before they have a pretty casual confession scene with each other. I mean they're fucking by episode 7 or whatever so needless to say that show moves at a breakneck pace. And this is what I'm getting at, these shows can't just throw a confession at you, they need to lay the ground-work so that it has weight when it finally happens. 

### 3. The Second Prettiest Girl
The show that inspired this train of thought was I Made Friends with the Second Prettiest Girl in My Class, a currently airing show which just dropped episode 5 a few days ago. In that episode Umi (the titular girl) confesses to Maki, the MMC, in what in my opinion is a rather underwhelming and out of left field way. 

The majority of the episode was focused on Umi's past and her complex about Yuu (The 1st prettiest girl in class, and her best friend) inadvertently stealing people from her. There was a touching moment between Umi and Maki, but I did not think at all that a confession was coming. She kinda just went for it out of nowhere. She didn't even ask him to respond, which could unfortunately mean that this actually drags out in an ambiguous way for a couple of episodes. 

But regardless of how it plays out, what shocked me a little was people's reaction to it. Frankly, people ate that shit up. They couldn't believe they were getting a confession so early, and they were delighted. At least that was the majority perspective on the Reddit and Twitter threads I saw on the subject. And I just could not get behind that. I think it was too early, this is what I was getting at earlier about how friends-to-lovers should play out. The attraction needs to be irresistible, and then when the denial can't possibly go on a second further without being ridiculous, then a confession happens. And I don't think we reached that point yet. I wanted to see them openly flirting around Yuu more, and for her to actively try to push them together despite their best efforts to resist. 

But this anime is literally currently airing, so I don't know how this will play out. Maybe it will all retroactively make sense, and in that case I'll own up to jumping the gun on accusing the show of doing it's confession too early. But for now this is where my thoughts are at.
