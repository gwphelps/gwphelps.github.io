## Kamiina Botan: The Anime for Anime Fans

Kamiina Botan is a show I keep finding myself wanting to talk about. Specifically it's a show that makes me think of what it means for something to be "art" vs a more bland, commercial venture. I think people are under the impression that for a show or movie to be art, it needs to tackle difficult subject matters, explore deep themes, or do something unprecedented. While I think these things can and do contribute to art, you can find art in more ways than just that. 

### Passion is key
Kamiina Botan's premise is not groundbreaking, unless you consider the premise of a handful of lesbians living in a college dorm together and falling in love new and original. It's a pretty standard CGDCT premise. What separates it from other similar series is the passion on display from the staff working on the show. It could have been so easy to make another mediocre seasonal CGDCT with standard animation and standard execution. But the staff behind the show are clearly trying to make something greater than that. 

The show has a pretty explicitly experimental visual style, with a different animation director commandeering the style decisions for each individual episode. There is little consideration for visual consistently between episodes, they are working purely off of self-contained, stylistic concepts. Some of the episodes look absolutely gorgeous, while some find themselves flying a little too close to the sun stylistically (looking at you episode 3). 

But you can see the passion, you can see the attempt by the staff involved to not just make an anime, but to make something great. And whether they succeed or fail, that choice is what makes Kamiina Botan art to me. And not just to me, for me as well.

### The Pinnacle of Trope
After becoming obsessed with The Monogatari Series, I've become interested in the concept of greatness within genre. Many people decry tropes as things to be eliminated from an anime in order to make it better. But Monogatari asks an ambitious question: What if we made a light novel series with all the trashy tropes you are used too, but made something actually great using them? Monogatari is not satire or parody. It can be a little tongue-in-cheek at times, but it is wholly embracing the tropes it engages with, and makes a great narrative on top of them. 

To me Kamiina Botan is in a similar vein. It has the audacity to try to be an experimental art piece on the backdrop of a CGDCT concept. It's not just looking at trope as a means to get viewers, it's actively trying to build something innovative and great on top of that starting point. And I think that's admirable.

To me this is an essential aspect of anime that some people can't stand. They think because an anime engages with tropes, that it's lazy or uninspired. But we're all anime fans here, right? We all understand these tropes like tsundere's, cultural festivals, running with toast in your mouth, etc. If you watch enough anime you simply must become able to enjoy the many, many iterations of those concepts, or it will be difficult to find things you enjoy in this medium. In fact, some of them you may love a lot. Tropes exist because people like them, and if you really like anime as a medium then surely some of these tropes work on you as well. 

### Trope + Passion = Potential Anime Greatness
Like the viewers, the people making these shows clearly enjoy these tropes as well. While there are many visionaries who insist on making anime that try to deviate from trope to a large extent (e.g. Studio Ghibli), many of the people in this industry are fans of the same tropes as their audience. The people making Kamiina Botan clearly enjoy CGDGT concepts, or else they would not be making this show the way they are. As a more niche concept, CGDGT shows are not for everybody, and that's fine. But for people who like that kind of show, isn't it great that they have people willing to try to elevate the concept they love so much into something greater than another generic derivative of the same premise, while still retaining the essence of what makes that trope enjoyable to the viewer in the first place?
