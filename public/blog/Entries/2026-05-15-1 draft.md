
## Karen Bee - Karen, Koyomi, and Justice

This is the first of what will probably be a lot of entries I do on The Monogatari Series. They are not in a particular order, I just felt like doing a piece on this arc because I recently re-watched it and it was on my mind. 

Nisemonogatari is a pretty controversial installment in The Monogatari series. A lot of people deride it as one of the worst parts of the show due to a slow and somewhat convoluted plot, as well as containing some of the most heinous ecchi content in the entire series, which is saying something. But Nise is extremely important, the concepts it introduces are the foundation for exploring Koyomi's character in the rest of the series. 

Right now I want to talk about the Karen Bee arc. Karen Bee is the longest individual arc in Monogatari, and as such is an arc that is split between a Senjougahara focus and a Karen focus, indicated by which character's theme plays at the beginning of the episode. Right now I want to focus on the Karen portion of the arc. 

A large focus in the arc is how Koyomi views Karen. He sees her as an immature little kid, prancing around town fighting bad guys and being some kind of superhero. In their first interaction in the arc he mocks her for the chuuni way she behaves (I'm not sure if you can use chuuni as an adjective, as a matter of fact I only learned the word like a year ago), and clearly has no regard for the validity of this heroic tale she is going to be able to tell him about.

Karen also says something interesting in this interaction: She states that Koyomi is really talking about himself. This is not the only time this happens in Nise, and is a pretty explicit indicator of how, to Koyomi, Karen functions as a reflection of himself. His dismissal of her is also a rejection of some of the aspects of himself that he views as childlike: namely his sense of justice. 

### External vs Internal Validation
One of the more confusing aspects of this arc for me is the idea of selfish vs selfless justice, something which comes to the forefront in the finale when Karen fights Koyomi. Koyomi argues that, because Karen fights for other people rather than herself, that her justice is not "real", essentially meaning that instead of doing good things because of an internal sense of right and wrong, Karen is doing good things for the sake of others. This is a rather nuanced point that I have a hard time unpacking but the issue with this to Koyomi is that the motivation is external rather than internal, making it "fake". He has a very poignant line where he says that Karen "fights villains, not wrongdoers." He claims that as long as her actions are self-sacrificial rather than self-satisfying, she will remain an imposter. 

As far as I can tell this issue with external influence is related to Koyomi's view on relationships mentioned in Kizu: That friends make you weaker as a human. by tying yourself to other people you are bound by their whims. When they are happy, you are happy. When they are sad, you are sad. Araragi does not want to be influenced by other people, and views the purity that comes from isolation as staying true to yourself. 

I dont' think Koyomi views his actions throughout the series so far as enacting justice. Clearly there are some things he actually feels that some actions he took were straight up immoral. Choosing to save Kiss-Shot the first time was not a decision made by some sense of justice, it was a desire to save someone, because that's Koyomi's dilemma, that he'll save anyone. Choosing to turn Kiss-Shot into a husk of a vampire because he didn't have it in his heart to kill her. A person of pure moral character would surely kill a vampire who is asking to die. 

I think the confusing part for me is that Koyomi does things for other people in a self-serving way, whereas he wants to enact justice because it's the right thing to do, without the external connection involved. He's essentially trying to be a hero as well. 

A confusing aspect of the series to me is how Koyomi and Karen differ, and how exactly Koyomi relates to the speech he gives Karen. One thing I'm certain on is that he does not think of himself as a good, moral person. He helps people in a selfish way, caring more about being someone's savior than doing the right thing. His actions are self-serving but his motivation is to save other people. I think this is the same as Karen, who despite callling herself a hero of justice, seems to care more about being a hero than actually doing justice. She goes to Kaiki with no plan at all, her only plan going in was to threaten to punch Kaiki, and presumably convince him to stop what he's doing. 

The primary difference between Karen and Koyomi is the awareness. Koyomi acknowledges that he doesn't really care about justice, he just wants to save people in a self-glorifying way. Karen also does this, as Koyomi states, but she isn't self-aware enough to acknowledge it. She feels like she is being self-sacrificial, helping others for their own sake. Ultimately I think the culmination of the Karen Bee arc is Araragi accepting Karen's disposition, and in turn accepting his own selfish desire to save people. You don't have to always adhere to this strict code of ethics, sometimes  you can just save the person in front of you. This is an idea brought up in Mayoi Jiangshi as well, where Koyomi corrects Shinobu by saying that he is not saving the world, he is just saving a girl. I don't think he would have been able to say that without the development he has in Nise. 


To help break down Koyomi's views on justice and morality as portrayed in Nise I'm going to explore these ideas on two axis': Selfish vs Selfless, and Justice vs Heroism. 
### Justice vs Heroism
The big question of this dichotomy is a matter of morality: Are you acting because you believe you are doing the right thing, or are you acting out of an emotional drive to help someone in need? This to me is the most important thing to understand to really get what bothers Koyomi about both himself and Karen. Regarding Karen, Koyomi believes that she is acting out of concern for her classmates, rather than truly acting on her own moral compass. That is why he calls her an imposter, her justice is fake. It's just a facade, underneath which is an irrational, emotional drive to help people. 

This is also how Koyomi views himself, and arguably how we are supposed to view him too. Through Bake and Kizu up to now Koyomi has made it abundantly clear that helping people to him is not about justice. Probably his most profound demonstration of this was when he decided to save Kiss-Shot in Kizu. No person operating on any level of justice or morality would allow a vampire to kill them in order to save the vampire. 

But this character trait is something that very much gnaws at Koyomi. He wishes he could be someone who is truly "A man of justice" as Senjougahara explicitly calls him in this arc. He doesn't view himself that way, and he doesn't view Karen that way either. 

The way I view it is essentially the community member vs the political ideologue. The community member is someone who is first and foremost concerned with their family and their community. The political ideologue is someone who has grand ideas about how society ought to be. 
### Self-Satisfaction vs Self-Sacrifice
This is the aspect that really gets me running in circles in my head trying to grasp it so here goes nothing: The main difference between Karen and Koyomi is that Karen is acting for other people, while Koyomi is acting for his own self-satisfaction. The self-sacrificial person wants to be the martyr, first they do the work, and then the people around them praise them for it. That is the validation loop. And this is what Koyomi believes Karen is doing. Her motivation is external, she acts this way in order to receive validation from others. 

Koyomi is different. As we have seen at various points, Koyomi's desire to help does not come from the validation of others, even the people he's trying to help. Here's an example: In Suruga Monkey, Koyomi is willing to allow Kanbaru to kill him if it would revert her back to normal and keep her original arm intact. This is not a decision that would earn him the respect or admiration of the people around him. Frankly many people, Kanbaru included, would be absolutely distraught at his decision. But that's ok, because it's not for them that he's doing it, it's for him. He's not doing the right thing, and he's also not trying to make them revere him, he's just trying to save them for his own self-fulfillment. This is the essence of Koyomi Araragi.

So what am I trying to say now? Essentially what I wanted to do was clarify my understanding of Koyomi's view on justice, and how he and Karen are the same, and how they are different. 



Claim 1: Koyomi and Karen are both people who act out their morals emotionally and impulsively, rather than originating from a strict moral code. 

Claim 2: Koyomi and Karen differ in that Koyomi does not help other people for the sake of the people he is saving, wheras Karen does. He does it for the self-fulfillment he finds in the action.
