
## Oshi No Ko: Tragedy or just edgy?

This discussion will be just about the anime, but with the knowledge that I have already been spoiled on one aspect of how the manga ends, as well as the consensus quality of the unadapted section of the manga, so that may influence my perspective. 

Oshi No Ko is an anime that I very much have a love/hate relationship with. On the one hand, I think the show has a ton going for it: A compelling premise, social commentary, interesting and complicated characters, incredible music and art/animation, among other things. It's a show I greatly enjoyed seasons one and two of, but have begun to sour on a bit in season 3. Because while the show has really good things going for it, there is one aspect I have never really cared for: The revenge plotline. And most of my issues with season 3 are related to the decision to focus more on this plotline over the character work they were doing in the previous seasons. 

A lot of my issues with the series are related to Aqua/The Doctor. Kana and Akane have always been compelling characters to me, which is probably why I liked season 2 so much. Ruby got more run in season 3 after being benched for most of season 2 but she's a whole other can of worms. But Aqua has always been kind of an enigma. The big question I have had about Aqua throughout the series is whether or not they intend to rehabilitate him. Because even on a surface level he's not a great guy. He is always going on about how he needs to use and manipulate other people to get what he wants, which is revenge on his father. 

There is no doubt that he is a tortured, traumatized individual. They even go into that in season 2, though it never comes up again funnily enough, and was not mentioned before season 2 either. Am I suggesting that the PTSD plotline was ham-fisted in without proper set up in order to give Aqua something to do during season 2? Yes. I have a suspicion we will not see any of Aqua's deliberate tortured acting in season 4, but we might. But my point is that as a character he's really all over the place. Sometimes he's cutthroat and manipulative, and sometimes he's very caring to the people around him, particularly Kana, who even when he was dating Akane he was very nice to (We've since learned that Kana looks almost exactly like Serina did before she lost her hair, so that's... something). And certainly characters are allowed to be nuanced, if you want to call Aqua that. Aqua is allowed to be both manipulative and caring depending on the situation. 

I guess if I were to try to ask one question to the author, should we want Aqua to succeed at getting his revenge, or not? Because to me the answer should obviously be no, getting revenge should not be Aqua's goal at all. He needs to decide what he wants out of this new life he has been given and do that. But the thing about the series that is giving me pause is that the author might answer yes to that question based on the events of season 3. 

In a different series I might give the author time to "cook" so to speak. The point we are at in the plot is not doomed by any means. There is still room for Aqua to get his revenge, and then feel aimless on what to do after that, and maybe find that revenge does not give him the catharsis he was looking for. The problem, is that from everything I've heard online, season 3 is pretty much the last "good" part of the series, and the quality takes a substantial nosedive shortly after this point. Which leaves me to believe that Aka Akasaka did not actually have a grand plan for Aqua, that he actually was just making it up as he went. Which is disappointing because this show has so much excellent set up for interesting character arcs that I have a feeling are not going to amount to anything. 

Another related train of thought is, "Why does Aqua need to have a happy ending?" This is a point I bring up on the assumption that Aqua has essentially given up on trying to live his life during season 3, committing fully to the revenge, and that's ultimately how it's going to play out. Tragedies are certainly valid forms of storytelling. But I just think that allowing Aqua to succumb to the darkness is not a great way to end it. I think to do a tragedy you really need to have a big, overarching point to it, more so than with a happen ending in my opinion. What exactly is the cautionary tale here, if any? 

Ultimately, I think it's kind of pointless to go too far into this train of thought, since I don't actually know what happens in the remaining portion of the series. More than anything I just want to voice a concern I have with the direction the series is choosing to go, leaning into the revenge plotline rather than focusing on Aqua's rehabilitation. I wish Aqua would have felt a little more torn on choosing to refocus his life on finding his father. When he decided that he was done trying to find his father, believing him to already be dead, he seemed the happiest he'd been in the entire series. And as soon as he found out that the information didn't make sense, he immediately went back to his old ways. There was never actually any real resolve there from him to move on from it, he just had no choice since he believed the case was closed.
